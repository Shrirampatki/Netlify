01DEFINITION

What Makes A Team Agile

Agile isn't a ceremony schedule — it's a set of working conditions that let a team learn and adapt fast.

1

Cross-Functional

The team holds every skill needed to ship value — design, build, test, release — without waiting on outside hand-offs.

2

Self-Organizing

The team decides how work gets done. Leaders set direction and outcomes, not task-by-task instructions.

3

Iterative

Work ships in small, frequent increments so feedback arrives while it's still cheap to act on.

4

Customer-Focused

Priorities are set by real user and business value, re-checked constantly rather than locked in upfront.

02CORE VALUES

The Values That Guide Decisions

When trade-offs come up, agile teams consistently lean toward the item on the left.

A

People & Interactions over rigid process

Process should serve the team's communication, not replace it.

B

Working Output over exhaustive documentation

A tested increment proves progress more reliably than a plan on paper.

C

Customer Collaboration over fixed contracts

Stakeholders stay engaged throughout, not just at kickoff and delivery.

D

Responding To Change over following a fixed plan

The plan is a hypothesis. New information is allowed to change it.

03TEAM ROLES

Who Does What

Three roles, three distinct jobs — none of them "manager of the others."

PO

Product Owner

Owns the backlog and its priority order. Represents the customer and business, and decides what "done" is worth building next.

SM

Scrum Master / Coach

Protects the team's process, removes blockers, and coaches the team toward better ways of working — not a task assigner.

DT

Development Team

Cross-functional group that designs, builds, tests, and delivers the work. Self-organizes around how to meet each goal.

05DAILY BEHAVIORS

How Agile Teams Should Actually Work

The ceremonies are containers. These habits are what actually make the work agile.

✓

Small Batches

Break work into pieces small enough to finish and validate within a single sprint — not months-long threads.

✓

Radical Transparency

Status, blockers, and risk are visible the moment they're known — no saving bad news for the review.

✓

Collective Ownership

The whole team owns the sprint goal together, not just whoever's name is on a ticket.

✓

Fast Feedback Loops

Show working increments to real stakeholders often, and let their reaction change next sprint's plan.

✓

Sustainable Pace

Commit to a pace the team can hold sprint after sprint — agile is a marathon cadence, not a sprint to burnout.

✓

Inspect & Adapt

Every retrospective produces at least one real change to how the team works, not just a list of complaints.

06WATCH FOR THIS

Common Pitfalls To Avoid

Teams that run the ceremonies but skip the mindset end up with "agile theater."

!

Zombie Ceremonies

Standups become status reports to a manager instead of a sync between teammates; retros happen but nothing changes after.

!

Proxy Product Owner

The PO isn't empowered to actually decide priority, so every choice bounces back up a chain of approvals.

!

Story-Point Gaming

Points get used to judge individual performance, so estimates inflate and the number stops meaning anything.

!

Over-Committed Sprints

The backlog is sized by hope, not by the team's real, measured capacity — burnout and missed goals follow.

07SUMMARY

The Short Version

Small, cross-functional teams that own their work end-to-end.
Short, frequent cycles with real feedback — not big-bang delivery.
Transparency and self-organization over top-down task assignment.
Every retrospective changes something — agility is a practiced habit.
